CPA, CPC, CTR and conversion rate: which metrics actually matter?

By Rodrigo Kato6 min readUpdated September 2026
The short answer

Four terms trip people up: CPC (cost per click), CTR (click-through rate), conversion rate (orders ÷ clicks) and CPA (cost per sale). For eCommerce, only two are the scoreboard: CPA and ROAS, the money metrics. CPC, CTR and conversion rate are diagnostics that explain the money, not goals to chase. A high CTR with a bad conversion rate is not a win.

Google Ads throws a lot of acronyms at you, and it is easy to end up optimising the wrong one. This is a plain-English glossary of the five metrics that matter most for an online store, what each actually means, and, more usefully, which ones are the goal and which are just clues.

The five metrics, defined

MetricWhat it isRole
CPC (cost per click)What you pay for one click. Spend ÷ clicks.Diagnostic
CTR (click-through rate)How often people click when they see your ad. Clicks ÷ impressions.Diagnostic
Conversion rate (CVR)How often a click becomes a sale. Conversions ÷ clicks.Diagnostic
CPA (cost per acquisition)What it costs to get one sale. Spend ÷ conversions.Goal
ROAS (return on ad spend)Revenue for every euro spent. Revenue ÷ spend.Goal

How they connect (the chain)

These metrics are not independent, they are links in one chain from an ad being seen to money in the bank:

Impressions → (CTR) → clicks → (CPC pays for them) → (conversion rate) → sales → CPA & ROAS.

Read left to right, each metric explains the next. If your CPA is too high, the cause is upstream: either your CPC is high (clicks are expensive), your conversion rate is low (clicks are not turning into sales), or both. That is exactly why CPC, CTR and CVR are worth watching, not as targets, but as the diagnostic trail that tells you why your CPA and ROAS look the way they do.

Goals vs diagnostics: the distinction that matters

This is the whole point of the guide. CPA and ROAS are goals, they measure whether the account makes money. CPC, CTR and conversion rate are diagnostics, they help you understand and improve the goals, but they are not themselves the target.

The trap to avoid: optimising a diagnostic as if it were a goal. A campaign can have a beautiful CTR and still lose money if those clicks do not convert. A very low CPC is worthless if the traffic is junk. Always chase a profitable CPA and ROAS, and use the other three to work out how to get there.

Common misreadings

  • “Our CTR is up, things are working.” Not necessarily. CTR up with conversion rate down can mean you are attracting more clicks that do not buy.
  • “We got the CPC down, great.” Only if the cheaper clicks still convert. Cheap, low-intent clicks can raise your CPA even as CPC falls.
  • “Conversion rate dropped, the ads are broken.” Maybe, but a conversion-rate drop is often a site, price or tracking issue, one of the usual suspects behind a falling ROAS, not an ad problem at all.

So what should you actually report?

Lead with the money: revenue, ROAS against your break-even, and CPA. Keep CPC, CTR and conversion rate as the second layer that explains movements in the first. If your reporting is built the other way round, with impressions and CTR as the headline, you are measuring effort rather than outcome, which is also a sign worth reading in an agency's reporting.

Frequently asked

What is CPA in Google Ads?

CPA (cost per acquisition, sometimes called cost per action) is what it costs you to get one conversion. It is your ad spend divided by the number of conversions: spend €1,000 and get 50 sales, and your CPA is €20. For eCommerce it is one of the two metrics that actually matter, because it ties spend directly to a result, alongside ROAS.

What is a good CTR, CPC or conversion rate for eCommerce?

There is no universal benchmark, because all three vary hugely by niche, price point, brand strength and campaign type. More importantly, they are diagnostics rather than goals. A good CTR or CPC is one that produces a profitable CPA and ROAS; a high CTR with a terrible conversion rate is not a win. Judge these three by whether they lead to a profitable cost per sale, not against an industry average.

What is the difference between CPA and ROAS?

Both measure efficiency, from different angles. CPA is the cost to get one conversion (spend divided by conversions), which suits businesses where every sale is worth roughly the same. ROAS is revenue divided by spend, which suits eCommerce where order values vary, because it accounts for how much each sale is worth, not just how many you got. Most stores steer primarily by ROAS and watch CPA alongside it.

Which Google Ads metric matters most for eCommerce?

The money metrics: ROAS (measured against your break-even ROAS) and CPA. Those tell you whether the account is profitable. CPC, CTR and conversion rate are diagnostics that explain why ROAS and CPA are where they are, useful for the person optimising, but not the scoreboard. If a report leads with impressions and CTR, it is measuring activity, not results.
