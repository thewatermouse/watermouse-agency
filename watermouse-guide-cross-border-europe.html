Cross-border Google Ads: expanding eCommerce across Europe

By Rodrigo Kato9 min readUpdated October 2026
The short answer

Expanding Google Ads across Europe is not "duplicate the campaign and change the country." Each market is its own language, currency, payment habit, competitor set and season. Run one structure per market, localise the feed and ads for real (not auto-translate), and judge each country on profit after shipping and returns, not on-screen ROAS. Expand where your product, language and logistics already fit, then widen.

Europe looks like one market on a map and behaves like twenty in a Google Ads account. Brands that treat it as a single blob, one campaign, one language, one bid, one feed, usually find their budget pooling in whichever country is cheapest to win, not the one that makes the most money. Here is what actually changes when you cross a border, and how to structure for it.

What actually changes per market

Five things differ country to country, and each one can quietly decide whether a market is profitable:

  • Language and search terms. Not just translation, the words people type. A literal translation of your best English keyword often isn't what Germans or Dutch shoppers actually search.
  • Currency and price perception. Prices must show in local currency, and what feels "premium" or "cheap" differs by market.
  • Payment methods. iDEAL in the Netherlands, invoice and Klarna in Germany, cards and PayPal in the UK. Missing the local method tanks conversion no matter how good the ad is.
  • Competitors and price comparison. Germany leans heavily on Idealo and price-comparison; the UK is Shopping-led and fiercely price-competitive at peak.
  • Seasonality and trust signals. Sinterklaas in the Netherlands, different Black Friday intensity, local review platforms (Trusted Shops in DE) and delivery expectations.

How to structure the account

The principle is simple: one market, one controllable unit. In practice:

  • Separate campaigns (or at least ad groups) per country, so language, budget, bids and schedules fit each market instead of being averaged.
  • A localised feed per market, titles, prices and availability in the local language and currency. This is the single biggest lever in Performance Max and Shopping, see our feed optimisation guide.
  • Market-specific landing pages, in-language, with the local payment and delivery promise visible.
  • Geo and language targeting set deliberately, target the country and the language; don't let a single campaign spill across borders by accident.
Rule of thumb: if you can't set a different budget, bid and feed for a market, you're not really running that market, you're letting Google decide how much of your money each country gets.

The mistakes that waste budget

  • Auto-translating ads and feed. Reads as foreign, misses real search terms, converts worse.
  • One blended ROAS target across markets. Hides the market that's losing money behind the one that's winning.
  • Ignoring fulfilment economics. A market can show a great ROAS and still lose money once cross-border shipping, duties and returns are counted.
  • Launching everywhere at once. Spreads budget and attention too thin to learn anything. Expand one market at a time.

When not to expand

Cross-border is a multiplier, not a fix. If your home market isn't profitable yet, another country won't rescue the account, it'll just lose money in a second language. Expand when your unit economics are solid at home (know your break-even ROAS), your logistics can actually serve the new market, and you can localise properly. Otherwise you're exporting a leak.

Frequently asked

One campaign for all of Europe, or one per country?

One per market, as a rule. Each country has its own language, currency, competitors and seasonality, so a single pan-European campaign averages that into mush and spends where it's cheapest, not most profitable. Separate campaigns and feeds per market let you fit each one.

Can I just auto-translate my ads and feed?

No. Machine translation reads as foreign, converts worse and misses the words locals search. Have a native speaker review titles, ad copy and key pages. Localisation is search terms, currency, tone and trust, not just swapped words.

What's the biggest hidden cost of cross-border?

Fulfilment and returns, not ads. Shipping times, duties, local payment methods and return logistics decide real profitability. A market can look great on ROAS and still lose money once cross-border shipping and returns are counted.

Which markets are easiest to enter first?

Usually English-first markets (UK, Ireland, Netherlands) and ones near your warehouse. Germany is large and lucrative but needs native German, local payment and price-comparison presence. Start where your product, language and shipping already fit.
