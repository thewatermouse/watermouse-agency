How break-even ROAS works

ROAS (return on ad spend) is revenue divided by ad spend. Your break-even ROAS is the point where the gross profit on a sale exactly covers the cost of the ad that produced it, no profit, no loss. The maths is simple:

Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ gross margin. A 40% margin needs a ROAS of 2.5; a 25% margin needs 4.0; a 60% margin needs only about 1.7. The thinner your margin, the harder your ads have to work just to stand still.

If you also entered an average order value, the tool shows your break-even CPA (cost per acquisition), which is simply your gross profit per order, the most you can pay for a sale before it costs you money. And if you entered a target profit, it shows the higher ROAS you would need to keep that slice of each sale after ad spend.

Three things this number hides

Break-even ROAS is the right floor to set, but treat it as a floor, not a plan:

  • It ignores other costs. Shipping, payment fees, returns and overheads all sit below gross margin, so real break-even is usually a bit higher than this.
  • It ignores repeat purchases. If customers come back, a first order at break-even can still be very profitable over their lifetime, which is why some brands deliberately run acquisition near break-even.
  • It is an average. Different products and markets have different margins; a blended target can quietly lose money on your thin-margin lines while winning on the fat ones.

Frequently asked

What is break-even ROAS?

Break-even ROAS is the return on ad spend at which the gross profit from a sale exactly equals its advertising cost, so you make no profit and no loss. It equals 1 divided by your gross margin: a 40% margin gives a break-even ROAS of 2.5.

How do I calculate break-even ROAS?

Divide 1 by your gross margin as a decimal. A 50% margin gives 1 ÷ 0.5 = 2.0; a 25% margin gives 1 ÷ 0.25 = 4.0. The lower your margin, the higher the ROAS you need just to avoid losing money.

What is a good ROAS for eCommerce?

Any figure comfortably above your break-even ROAS. There is no universal target: a 60%-margin brand can profit below a ROAS of 2, while a 20%-margin brand needs above 5 just to break even. Set targets against your own break-even, not an industry average.

Should I set my target ROAS at break-even?

No, break-even is the floor, not the goal. Set your target above it so each sale leaves a profit, and remember ROAS ignores shipping, returns, overheads and repeat purchases. Use break-even as the line you must stay above.
