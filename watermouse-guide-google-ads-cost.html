How much does Google Ads management cost for eCommerce?

By Rodrigo Kato8 min readUpdated September 2026
The short answer

Most European eCommerce brands pay for Google Ads management one of three ways: a percentage of ad spend (typically 10–20%), a flat monthly fee (commonly ~€1,000–€5,000+ depending on account size), or an in-house hire (€60k–€90k+/yr fully loaded). The percentage model pays the agency more when you spend more — which is why a flat fee is usually better aligned with your profit.

If you are a European eCommerce brand trying to work out what Google Ads management should cost, the honest answer is: it depends far less on the size of the agency and far more on the pricing model and who actually does the work. Below is how the three common models really compare, what drives the number, and how to sanity-check any quote you are given.

The three ways you'll be charged

Almost every quote you receive will fall into one of these three structures. Each changes the incentives behind your account, not just the invoice.

What actually drives the price

Two accounts spending the same amount can justify very different fees. The real cost drivers are:

  • Account size and spend — more budget means more testing, more structure, and more risk to manage.
  • Number of markets and languages — running NL, UK and DE well is three markets' worth of work, not one.
  • Feed and catalogue complexity — a 5,000-SKU Shopping feed needs far more attention than a 30-product range.
  • Channel mix — Search-only is lighter than a full Search + Shopping + Performance Max + YouTube build.
  • Reporting and involvement — weekly senior oversight and honest analysis cost more than an automated monthly dashboard.

Why percentage-of-spend quietly works against you

The percentage model is the industry default, and it contains a structural conflict: your agency is paid more precisely when it spends more of your money. There is rarely a strong incentive to cut wasted spend, consolidate, or tell you that you are over-invested in a channel — because doing so lowers their own fee.

A flat fee removes that conflict. Whether the right move this month is to scale up or to pull back, the fee is the same, so the advice you get is about your profit, not the agency's revenue.

Rule of thumb: management fees should fall as a share of spend as you grow. A €1,500 flat fee is ~15% of €10k/mo spend, but only ~3% of €50k/mo. A percentage model keeps taking the same cut no matter how large — and how efficient — your account becomes.

What you should actually be paying for

Price is only meaningful against what is delivered. The things worth paying for are:

  • Senior time on the account — the person making daily decisions should have run accounts like yours before.
  • Honest reporting — revenue, ROAS and CAC, with an explanation of why they moved — not impressions and clicks.
  • Structural work — feed quality, account architecture, bidding logic. The unglamorous work that compounds.

What you should not be paying senior rates for: a junior learning on your budget while a senior name appears only on the contract and the quarterly call.

Red flags in a pricing conversation

  • “You'll have a team” — often means a junior does the work and you rarely speak to the senior you were sold.
  • Long lock-ins — a 12-month contract with heavy exit penalties protects the agency, not your results.
  • Reporting built on vanity metrics — if the proposal leads with impressions and CTR, that is what they will optimise for.
  • A fee that scales with spend but not with value — see above.

So what should you budget?

A sensible way to frame it: decide your monthly ad spend first, then treat management as a layer on top that should be a reasonable share of that spend at your size. At smaller spends a flat fee will look like a larger percentage; as you scale, that same fee becomes a smaller and smaller share — which is exactly the direction the maths should run. If a quote does the opposite (taking a fixed percentage that never shrinks), you are paying more for the privilege of growing.

Frequently asked

How much does Google Ads management cost for eCommerce?

In Europe, management is usually priced as a percentage of ad spend (typically 10–20%), a flat monthly fee (commonly around €1,000–€5,000+ depending on account size and scope), or the fully-loaded cost of an in-house hire (€60k–€90k+ per year). Watermouse uses a flat fee of €1,500–€4,000/mo. The right number depends on your spend, number of markets, feed complexity and channel mix — not on the size of the agency.

Is a percentage of spend or a flat fee better?

A flat fee is usually better aligned with your interests. Percentage-of-spend pays the agency more when you spend more, which creates little incentive to cut waste or tell you when you are over-invested. A flat fee stays the same whether the right move is to scale up or pull back, so the advice you get is about your profit rather than the agency's revenue.

How much should I budget for management versus ad spend?

Set your ad spend first, then treat management as a layer on top. As a share of spend, a good flat fee should shrink as you grow — a €1,500 fee is around 15% of €10k/mo spend but only about 3% of €50k/mo. Be wary of any model where the management cut never falls as your account gets larger and more efficient.

Is a freelancer cheaper than an agency for Google Ads?

Often, yes — a senior freelancer or solo operator carries far less overhead than a traditional agency, so more of your fee goes into actual account work rather than layers of management. The trade-off is capacity and redundancy. We cover this in detail in our guide on choosing between an agency, a freelancer and hiring in-house.
