Google Ads management fee benchmark for European eCommerce (2026)

By Rodrigo KatoWatermousePublished October 2026Free to cite (CC BY 4.0)
The benchmark in one line

In 2026, European eCommerce brands typically pay between €350 and €6,000+ per month to manage Google Ads, depending almost entirely on ad spend and scope. The effective management fee starts around 12–18% of spend at small budgets and falls below 6% as accounts scale, if your fee does the opposite, you are paying to grow.

There is a lot of noise around what Google Ads management "should" cost, and very little of it is specific to European eCommerce. This page is our attempt to fix that: a plain-language benchmark of what brands actually pay in 2026, broken down by ad spend band, pricing model and market. It is free to cite, the methodology is at the bottom, and the underlying logic is the same one we use when we price an account ourselves.

This is the single most useful cut. Fees scale with the complexity that comes with larger budgets (more campaigns, more markets, more feed), but not linearly, which is why the effective percentage falls as you grow.

Why the effective % should fall: a €1,500 flat fee is ~15% of €10k/mo spend, but only ~3% of €50k/mo. A percentage model keeps taking the same cut no matter how large, or how efficient, your account becomes. For how we think about this, see our full cost guide.

What actually moves a quote up or down

Two brands spending the same amount can be quoted very differently. The real drivers are:

  • Number of markets and languages, running NL, UK and DE well is three markets' worth of work, not one.
  • Feed and catalogue complexity, a 5,000-SKU Shopping feed needs far more attention than a 30-product range.
  • Channel mix, Search-only is lighter than a full Search + Shopping + Performance Max + YouTube build.
  • Seniority on the account, a senior operator making daily calls costs more than a junior following a checklist, and is usually cheaper per euro of result.
  • Reporting depth, weekly revenue/ROAS/CAC analysis costs more than an automated monthly dashboard, and is worth it.

How to use this benchmark

Three practical ways to put it to work:

  • Sanity-check a quote. Find your spend band in Benchmark 1. If a quote is far above the range with no extra scope to justify it, or far below (a red flag for junior-only work), ask why.
  • Watch the direction of the percentage. As you scale, your effective management % should fall. If your current deal keeps the same cut regardless of size, you are paying more precisely for growing.
  • Separate price from value. The cheapest option that quietly burns budget is not cheap. Judge a quote against the seniority, reporting and structural work it actually includes.
Methodology

This benchmark reflects indicative 2026 market rates for European eCommerce Google Ads management, compiled from three inputs: publicly listed agency and freelancer rate information across European markets; prevailing day-rate and retainer levels for senior PPC specialists; and Watermouse's own engagements managing accounts for brands in the Netherlands, the UK, Germany and beyond.

Figures are presented as ranges, not precise averages, because real quotes vary with market, channel mix, feed complexity and seniority. They are intended as a sanity-check reference, not a formal survey of a fixed sample. Currency is euro unless noted; UK figures are typically quoted in GBP. Frequently asked

What is a typical Google Ads management fee in Europe in 2026?

For European eCommerce brands, typical fees run roughly €350–€900/mo for very small accounts, €900–€2,500/mo in the €10k–€30k spend band, and €2,500–€6,000+/mo above €50k in spend. As a percentage of ad spend, the effective fee usually starts around 12–18% at small budgets and falls below 6% as spend grows.

How much does it cost to manage a €20,000/month Google Ads budget?

At around €20,000/mo in ad spend, European eCommerce brands typically pay €1,200–€2,500/mo in management, whether as a flat fee or 8–15% of spend, roughly 6–12% of the budget. A flat fee at this level tends to be better aligned with your profit than a pure percentage.

Is a percentage of spend or a flat monthly fee more common in Europe?

Both are common. Traditional agencies most often charge a percentage of ad spend (typically 10–20%) or a tiered flat retainer. Senior freelancers and boutique operators more often use a flat monthly fee, which is usually better aligned with the client because it does not reward the manager for spending more of your budget.

Why are fees higher in the UK and DACH than in Southern Europe?

Fees track local senior-specialist labour costs and competition for talent. The UK, Germany, Switzerland, the Netherlands and the Nordics have higher prevailing rates than Spain, Italy, Portugal or much of Central and Eastern Europe, so the same scope is typically quoted 20–40% higher in the northern and DACH markets.
